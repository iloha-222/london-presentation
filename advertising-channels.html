Shambhala Times

Community blog

Audience & Relevance

Community news forum for the global Shambhala Buddhist network.

Readers are members of Shambhala centers (Tibetan Buddhism-derived). Relevant for reaching engaged practitioners, especially in Europe and North America. Content is community-driven.

Ad Options

No paid ads (not a commercial magazine).

Accepts article submissions and community announcements. Submit a piece about a course or event. If approved, it gets published as news. A free PR forum within the sangha.

Academic Tibetan Studies

H-Buddhism (Buddhist Scholars Network)

Email/forum via H-Net

Audience & Relevance

Global academic mailing list for Buddhist Studies scholars. Members include professors and grad students (many studying Tibetan Buddhism).

Highly relevant for reaching academically inclined learners or those in Tibetan Studies programs.

Ad Options

Announcement posts on the listserv via H-Net. Appropriate for announcing language courses, summer intensives, etc. Many past posts share program announcements.

Posts are moderated. Must be framed as academic resource or opportunity.

Cost

Free. H-Net is a nonprofit network. No fees to post academic announcements, assuming they meet guidelines.

How to Promote

Requires academic affiliation to subscribe/post.

If eligible, send our announcement to H-Buddhism via the H-Net interface. If not subscribed, find a list member to post on our behalf.

University Tibetan Studies Departments

Various: SOAS (UK), Columbia (USA), etc.

Audience & Relevance

University programs in Tibetan or Buddhist Studies (i.e., SOAS, Columbia, UVA) have students already learning Tibetan.

They often circulate info about external language programs or summer schools. Reaches serious learners.

Ad Options

Informal outreach: Request to post flyers on departmental bulletin boards or send an email to program coordinators to forward to students.

Some universities have newsletters or Facebook pages for their Asia/Himalaya centers (i.e., Colorado's Tibet Himalaya Initiative) where events/courses are announced.

Cost

Free to share via academic channels (with permission). Effort required to contact each department. No formal ad fees, though placement is not guaranteed.

How to Promote

Identify specific programs (i.e., SOAS Language Centre, Columbia's Modern Tibetan Studies Program) and email the department administrator or professors.

Provide a concise description and explain how it benefits their students. No centralized contact; handle individually. Not found in sources as a paid service.

Dharma Centers & Communities

Kagyu Samye Dzong (London)

Audience & Relevance

Local Tibetan Buddhist center (Kagyu tradition) in Europe. Offers meditation classes and Tibetan language classes for its sangha.

Dedicated practitioners in UK. Many interested in learning Tibetan for practice. Relevant because they value Tibetan for Dharma texts.

Ad Options

Center newsletter/website: Samye Dzong has a newsletter (see Subscribe on their site) and an events page.

They promote their own courses but may share a partner's course if it complements their offerings (especially if introduced by a trusted teacher). No formal ads, but community announcements at weekly meetings or on notice boards might be possible.

Cost

Free (as a community service announcement). Any cost would be offering a donation to the center or cross-promotion. No advertising fees for sharing info with sangha, if approved by center coordinators.

How to Promote

Approach the center director or education coordinator via email: info@london.samye.org

Offer to give a short intro session or provide a flyer for their notice board. Inclusion in the email newsletter at their discretion.

Lerab Ling Retreat Centre (Rigpa)

France

Audience & Relevance

Major Tibetan Buddhist retreat center in Europe (Nyingma/Rigpa).

International retreat attendees, many of whom are dharma students who might pursue Tibetan language to deepen practice. European audience (France/UK/Europe).

Ad Options

Sangha announcements: Lerab Ling and Rigpa newsletters sometimes mention allied courses or resources for students.

No commercial ad program. If our course has support of Rigpa teachers or is seen as beneficial, they might include it in a community email or on their website's news.

Possibly could host a session/introduction during one of their events.

Cost

No set cost. Promotion is relationship-based. Free if they decide to share (or if we rent space to host a class there, that would have a fee, but listing an external online course in their communications would be gratis).

How to Promote

Email the Lerab Ling education team or communications (contact info on site).

Explain the course and how it serves Rigpa students. Inquire if they can share it with their mailing list or allow a flyer at the center.

Local Dharma Centers (Europe/UK)

i.e., Jamyang Buddhist Centre London, Samye Dzong branches, etc.

Audience & Relevance

Many city Dharma centers in Europe have active communities of Buddhist practitioners who could be keen on Tibetan language (for scripture, mantra, etc.).

Audience is local but collectively sizable. These centers often know of translation programs and encourage study.

Ad Options

Community bulletin & newsletters: Most centers have email newsletters and notice boards.

While they mainly promote in-house courses, they occasionally mention external opportunities (especially if recommended by a resident teacher).

Classifieds or Sangha emails may also be available for members to share offerings. Requires personal connection or the center's endorsement.

Cost

Free, as it's not formal advertising. If the center agrees, they'll include it as a friendly mention. Some centers might politely decline to promote non-center events, whereas others are open if it benefits members.

How to Promote

Individually reach out to center staff. For example, Jamyang London or Edinburgh Samye Dzong. Email the center manager with details.

Provide a blurb they can easily drop into a newsletter. Building relationships (or getting a known teacher to mention the course) can help. No centralized process; handled case by case.

Tergar & Other Global Sanghas

Tergar, Shambhala, FPMT, etc.

Audience & Relevance

International practice communities (i.e., Tergar, Shambhala, FPMT) have online forums and Facebook groups for members.

Global (North America, Europe). Include many who might want Tibetan language skills. Highly targeted to engaged Dharma students.

Ad Options

Online community groups: For instance, Tergar has online discussion groups; Shambhala has local Facebook groups. Sharing our course in those groups (if allowed) can be effective word-of-mouth.

Some organizations have "Sangha Resources" pages on their websites listing external educational resources. We could request to be listed.

Cost

Free (community sharing). No formal cost, but don't spam. It should be a genuine resource offer. If listed on a resources page, no fee.

How to Promote

Join the relevant Facebook or online community group as a member and post about our course (following group rules). Or ask a community moderator if it's okay to share.

For official resource pages, email the organization's admin or web team with our information. (For example, Tergar Institute might consider listing under related courses).
